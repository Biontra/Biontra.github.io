Character consistency bench

TUTAR is a small benchmark that checks whether video models can keep a character intact while making that character do something. Five models, one character (Aikyo), 20 commands.

The human check did not pass, so the table below is not a firm ranking.

What it measures

It asks two questions and scores them separately: did the model do the command, and did it keep the character while doing it?

Input: one start image and 20 commands. The commands were written in Turkish; the models got an English translation. Each model got each command once: 100 attempts, 98 clips, 2 safety refusals.

It does not measure general video quality, audio or speed. Price is not part of the score; the chart shows credits per kept-character answer next to the scores. The judge looks only at frames; for the two models where audio could not be turned off, it was removed afterwards.

How it is scored

The judge is Claude. Code takes 10 frames per second from each video, plus a top-half and a bottom-half crop of every frame, and the judge answers from these frames.

There are 103 questions: 101 are scored and 2 only give information and do not count. 77 of the 103 were written by hand; the rest were written by AI or are unconfirmed readings. Each answer is yes, no, not visible or no judge answer.

A clip the model did not generate counts as not done in Did the command and is left out of Kept the character. I wrote this rule after the videos were made.

The questions, the answer key, the start image, the videos and the frames are private. If the questions were public, a model or provider could be tuned to their answers. In the open files, questions appear only by ID.

Findings

  • Doing the command and keeping the character are different skills. Kling 3.0 did every command (100%) and kept the character least (84.1%).
  • Seedance 2.0 Mini kept the character best (96.1%) and was the cheapest per correct character answer (0.34 credits). Gemini Omni Flash 1.2 was the most expensive (0.81).
  • Questions written for one specific command were the hardest for every model: all five score lower on them than on the questions asked for every clip.
  • Gemini Omni Flash 1.2 refused 2 of the 20 commands (safety filter). A refused command counts as not done.
  • The judge is not good enough to rank close models: it agreed with my hand scores on 80.2% of answers, and I had set 85% in advance.

Example questions

The benchmark questions are private. I wrote 60 more questions of the same kind for the shared NEXA benchmark; those are public and are not part of the private set. Two of the ones I wrote by hand:

  • A man with curly hair and a red sweater stands in a kitchen. Command: he opens the fridge, takes a tomato and bites it. The video must get right: he takes one tomato; juice runs from where he bit; the inside of the tomato is not broken.
  • Same man, same kitchen. Command: he cracks an egg into the pan. The video must get right: the egg white sets in a believable shape; the shell breaks properly; the egg flows properly.

All 60 public questions 10 written by hand, 50 synthetic

Human check: did not pass

I scored 20 clips by hand, chosen by code with a fixed seed, without seeing the judge's answers. Human and judge agreed on 80.2% of answers (275/343). The threshold I wrote down in advance was 85%, so the check did not pass.

Most of the gap comes from 39 answers the judge never gave in these 20 clips (2 of the 20 got no judge answers at all); each of them counts as a disagreement. Where the judge did answer, agreement is 90.5% (275/304). That is not the number I committed to, so it does not replace 80.2%.

So the table above is one judge's reading that matches a human 80.2% of the time. It is not a firm ranking.

Before scoring I had watched about 60 to 70 of the 98 videos, so my answers may have used things the frames do not show, such as motion or sound. The judge saw only frames.

Side test: local models as the judge

I also asked six open-source vision models on my laptop to answer 10 of the check questions. None beat a coin flip: the best got 5 of 10, and the Claude judge got 8 of 10 on the same questions. The local models saw 3 frames per clip, the judge saw about 50, so this is not an equal comparison.

Limits

One character, one start image and one try per command. 20 commands. Three of the five models are cheap tiers. A single frame-based judge, with no second judge.
